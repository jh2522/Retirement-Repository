BEHIND THE NUMBERS

How the calculators work

The calculations are designed to be understandable. Here is what happens to your money each year and what the results mean.

Balances use the start of each age

The graph and table show how much is in the portfolio at the beginning of each age. This keeps retirement ages, Life Expectancy, and ending-balance targets on the same timeline.

One year is calculated at a time

During working years, the calculator adds savings and growth. During retirement, it subtracts spending not covered by passive income and then applies investment growth.

Project My Retirement

This calculator follows the information you enter and projects your portfolio from your current age through your Life Expectancy.

During your working years

Your annual contribution is your salary multiplied by your savings rate. Salary then grows by your annual salary-growth assumption.

Next year's balance = current balance with a full year of growth + this year's contribution with approximately half a year of growth

The half-year of growth assumes that contributions are made gradually throughout the year rather than all on January 1 or December 31.

During retirement

Retirement spending is entered in today's dollars. It is increased for inflation between your current age and retirement, then increased again during every retirement year.

Passive income starts at the amount you enter during your first retirement year. It then grows with inflation each year. Examples could include Social Security, a pension, or income from investment properties.

Next year's balance = (current balance − spending not covered by passive income) × retirement growth

If passive income is greater than spending, the calculator does not add the extra income to the portfolio. It simply treats the portfolio withdrawal as $0.

Money Runs Out

This result shows the age during which the portfolio is depleted. For example, if you begin age 85 with money but the calculation leaves $0 for age 86, the box reports Age 85. If depletion happens during the Life Expectancy year, the result says Does not run out.

Earliest Sustainable Retirement

The calculator tests earlier retirement ages using the same information. It reports the earliest age that does not run out of money before the Life Expectancy year. If the retirement age you entered already runs out too early, this result displays N/A.

Find My Savings Rate

This calculator works backward. Instead of asking you to enter a savings rate, it estimates the minimum percentage of salary needed to reach your desired balance at the start of your Life Expectancy age.

It repeatedly tests different savings rates until it finds one that lands very close to your selected ending balance. The resulting projection uses the same salary growth, contribution timing, inflation, passive-income, spending, and investment-return rules described above.

  • If existing savings are already enough, the required rate is 0%.
  • If saving 100% of salary is not enough under the entered information, the result says more than 100%.
  • The retire-three-years-earlier result reruns the calculation with a retirement age three years younger.

What the projection cannot predict

The calculator uses the same average return every year. Real markets rise and fall, and poor returns near retirement can affect a portfolio differently from poor returns many years earlier.

The current model also does not account for taxes, investment fees, account-specific withdrawal rules, detailed Social Security rules, changing spending needs, or unexpected expenses. The results are useful for comparing scenarios, not predicting an exact future.

Remember: This calculator is an educational estimate based on the information you enter. Actual results will vary.
